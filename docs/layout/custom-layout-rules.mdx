This page specifies how a [`Layout`](custom-layout.mdx) sizes and places its children, and how a root `Layout` is sized. Each rule has a worked example, and each example is checked by a test, named after the rule, in [`CustomLayoutGuideConformanceTest`](https://github.com/facebook/litho/blob/master/litho-it/src/test/com/facebook/litho/customlayout/CustomLayoutGuideConformanceTest.kt). If a rule and Litho ever disagree, the test fails.

The rules follow a Yoga `Column`: a `Layout`'s child gets the box a `Column` gives the same child, and a root `Layout` the box a `Column` root gets. `CustomLayoutBoxConstraintsTest` and `CustomLayoutRootTest` compare the two across many styles in both directions. The differences kept on purpose are listed in [Where a Layout differs from Yoga](#where-a-layout-differs-from-yoga), each with its reason; flexbox item props are the other exception ([C10](#c-a-layouts-child)).

All sizes are in pixels. Unless an example says otherwise, a child's content is 30 x 25 and a root's is 30 x 20.

> **Rooms go down. Sizes come up. You place.**
> A child's own style beats the room; your parent has the last word on your size.

## The core model in five rules

Most questions are answered by these five rules. The numbered rules below give every detail and case.

1. **The layout function works in the content box**: the `Layout`'s own box less its padding and border ([P1](#p-measuring-and-placing)).
2. **A child's own style beats the room.** Its own `width`, `minWidth` or `maxWidth` wins over the room you measure it under; `measuredWidth` is what it took, and `width` is that kept within the room ([P2](#p-measuring-and-placing), [C3 to C7](#c-a-layouts-child), [C12](#c-a-layouts-child)).
3. **A child that doesn't fit is drawn from its start edge**, not centred ([P3](#p-measuring-and-placing)).
4. **The declared size is a request**: the parent may give the `Layout` another size ([P5, P7](#p-measuring-and-placing)).
5. **Only placed children appear**, drawn in the order they're placed, and the same room returns the same `Placeable` ([P6](#p-measuring-and-placing)).

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Room** | The `SizeConstraints` a layout function passes to `measure` for a child. On each axis it's **exact** (`min == max`), **at most** (`min` 0 and a bounded `max`), **unbounded** (no `max`), or any other range from a `min` to a `max`. |
| **Own style** | The size props the child sets on itself: `width`, `height`, `minWidth`, `maxWidth`, `minHeight`, `maxHeight`, their percentage forms, `aspectRatio`, `margin`, `padding` and `border`. |
| **Box** | The child's border box: its content plus its padding and border. |
| **`measuredWidth`** | What the child took: its box plus its margins. It may fall outside the room. |
| **`width`** | `measuredWidth` kept within the room. It's the space the child occupies in its parent's arithmetic. |
| **Fixed axis** | An axis the room makes exact, or that the child's own width or height decides. |
| **Content box** | A `Layout`'s own box less its padding and border: the space its layout function works in. |
| **Declared size** | The size a layout function passes to `layout(width, height)`. |
| **Built, mounted** | A placed child is built into the layout result and mounted on screen. A child that's measured but not placed is neither. Every declared child is still resolved, in `render`. |

`measuredHeight` and `height` are the same for the other axis.

## P. Measuring and placing

**P1. The layout function works in the content box.** The constraints it receives are the `Layout`'s own box less its padding and border. A minimum reaches it only on an exact axis; on any other axis its minimum is 0. `place` measures positions from the content box's top-left corner, and `placeRelative` from its top start corner.

> A `Layout` with `width(300)` and `padding(all = 10)` receives an exact width of 280. A child placed at (0, 0) lands at (10, 10) from the `Layout`'s edge.

**P2. `measure` returns a `Placeable`, whose `width` is kept within the room and whose `measuredWidth` is not.**

> A child with `width(150)`, measured at most 100 wide, has a `width` of 100 and a `measuredWidth` of 150.

**P3. When `measuredWidth` differs from `width`, the child is drawn from the start edge of the `width` it occupies.** It isn't centred. It overflows, or falls short of, the end edge: the right one left to right, the left one right to left.

> A child with `width(81)` in an exact room 100 wide sits at x = 0, 19 short of the end. A child with `width(150)` at most 100 wide sits at x = 0, 50 past the end. Right to left, a child with `width(120)` in an exact room 300 wide sits at x = 180.

To align a child to the end, place it by `measuredWidth`, not `width` (see [Common mistakes](custom-layout.mdx#common-mistakes)).

**P4. `placeRelative` mirrors in a right-to-left layout; `place` doesn't.** `placeRelative(start, top)` puts the child `start` from the container's start edge: right to left, that's `containerWidth - start - placeable.width` from the left, against the width the container ends up with. `place(x, y)` counts `x` from the left of the declared box. That box starts at the content box's start edge, as any box that doesn't fit does ([P3](#p-measuring-and-placing)), so it's the content box's left edge whenever the declared and final widths match. When they don't, in a right-to-left layout it's aligned to the right edge.

> - In a container 1000 wide, a 100-wide child at `placeRelative(start = 30)` sits at x = 30 left to right and x = 870 right to left.
> - At `place(x = 30)` it sits at x = 30 in both directions.
> - A right-to-left container that declares 100 and is stretched to 1000 puts the child at `place(0, 0)` or `placeRelative(0, 0)` at x = 900.
> - One that declares 1200 and is kept to 1000 by an exact room puts it at x = -200 with `place(0, 0)`, and at x = 900 with `placeRelative(0, 0)`.

**P5. The declared size is a request.** The parent may give the container another size; a stretching `Column` does. The placement block runs once the final size is known.

> A `Layout` that declares 100 x 100 inside a `Column` 1000 wide that stretches its children is 1000 wide.

**P6. Measuring again under the same constraints returns the same `Placeable`, only placed children are built and mounted, and paint order is placement order.** A child that is measured but not placed occupies no space. Overlapping children are drawn in the order they were placed, the last on top.

> A child measured twice at most 300 wide returns the same `Placeable` both times. Of two children measured, the one never placed doesn't appear in the result. Two children placed second-first are drawn second-first.

**P7. Under a Yoga parent, a `Layout`'s declared size can pass an at-most room**, within its own max, as a `Column` that wraps its content grows past it. An exact axis stays the room's. Under a `Layout` parent, [C12](#c-a-layouts-child) applies instead.

> A `Layout` whose function declares 400 wide, in a `Column` 300 wide that doesn't stretch it, is 400 wide; with `maxWidth(350)`, it's 350. In a `Column` 300 wide that stretches it, it's 300.

## C. A Layout's child

The rules apply per axis, within the room the child is measured under.

**C1. A percentage is of the room.** A percentage `width`, `height`, min or max is of the room's maximum on its own axis, and has no effect on an unbounded axis. A percentage `margin` or `padding` is of the room's width on both axes, as in CSS, and is 0 when the width is unbounded.

> In a room at most 200 wide, `marginPercent(top = 10)` is a top margin of 20. When the room is unbounded, `widthPercent(50)` and `minWidthPercent(50)` each keep the child's content width, 30, and `marginPercent(left = 10)` is 0.

**C2. Margins come out of the room.** Both of the room's bounds drop by them, so a negative margin raises both. `measuredWidth` includes the margins.

> A child with `width(100).margin(all = 7)` at most 300 wide has a `measuredWidth` of 114 and sits at x = 7. In an exact room 300 wide, `margin(horizontal = 10)` makes the child 280 wide, and `margin(horizontal = -10)` makes it 320.

**C3. An own min raises the room's minimum and never lowers it.** In an exact room the child still fills the room, as a `Column` child that's stretched does. A min above the room's maximum wins over it.

> `minWidth(200)` in an exact room 300 wide is 300. `minWidth(400)` at most 300 wide is 400.

**C4. An own max lowers the room's maximum and never raises it.** It wins over an exact room.

> `maxWidth(80)` in an exact room 300 wide is 80, at the start edge.

**C5. An own min above an own max wins**, as in CSS ([CSS 2 §10.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/CSS2/visudet.html#min-max-widths)).

> `minWidth(90).maxWidth(50)` is 90.

**C6. An own width or height fixes the axis, within the child's own min and max, whatever the room.**

> `width(80)` in an exact room 300 wide is 80. `width(80).minWidth(120)` is 120.

**C7. A percentage size fixes the axis too, and may exceed the room.** It's of the room's maximum ([C1](#c-a-layouts-child)).

> `widthPercent(50)` in an exact room 300 wide is 150. `widthPercent(150)` at most 300 wide has a `measuredWidth` of 450 and a `width` of 300.

**C8. An aspect ratio derives the other axis only when exactly one axis is fixed**, by an exact room or the child's own width or height. The derived axis is kept within its own min and max. With both axes fixed, or neither, the ratio has no effect; both cases differ from Yoga ([D4 and D5](#where-a-layout-differs-from-yoga)). A ratio that isn't a finite number above 0 counts as none.

> - In an exact room 200 wide, `aspectRatio(2f)` is 200 x 100; with `maxHeight(60)` too, it's 200 x 60.
> - `width(120).aspectRatio(3f)` is 120 x 40.
> - In an exact room 300 wide, `height(40).aspectRatio(2f)` is 300 x 40: both axes are fixed.
> - At most 300 wide, `aspectRatio(2f)` alone keeps its content's 30 x 25: neither axis is fixed.
> - In an exact room 200 wide, a ratio of `Float.NaN`, infinity, 0 or -2 keeps the content's height, 25.

**C9. Padding and border set the smallest the box can be.**

> `padding(all = 8)` in an exact room 10 x 10 is 16 wide, at the start edge.

**C10. Flexbox item props on a child have no effect.** A `Layout` doesn't run flexbox among its children; its layout function decides their sizes.

> A child with `width(100)` and `flex(grow = 1f)`, measured at most 1000 wide, is 100 wide. A child with `flex(basis = 200.px)` keeps its content width, 30.

**C11. A negative size or own max has no effect, and a negative own min acts as 0.** The axis is as if the prop weren't set, as a negative length is invalid in CSS ([CSS Sizing §3](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-sizing-3/#sizing-properties)).

> At most 300 wide, `width(-10)`, `widthPercent(-50)` and `minWidth(-10)` each keep the content width, 30. In an exact room 300 wide, `maxWidth(-10)` is 300.

**C12. A child's content stays within its room.** Only the child's own size, min, max, padding or border can take its box past the room ([C3](#c-a-layouts-child), [C6](#c-a-layouts-child), [C9](#c-a-layouts-child)); content larger than the room is kept to it.

> A `Layout` child whose function declares 400 wide, measured at most 300 wide, has a `width` and a `measuredWidth` of 300.

## R. A root Layout

A root `Layout` is one laid out on its own, with no parent laying it out: the root of a tree, or the content of a component that renders its content at layout time, such as `RenderWithConstraints`. Its holder, the view or the node that holds it, gives it room from 0 up to the holder's constraints on each axis, and a default for any size the root doesn't set itself.

A root follows the child rules, except that it isn't measured by a parent's function: it fills an exact axis and wraps otherwise (R1), its margins come out of an exact axis too (R2), its own width, height and min can pass its room (R3, R4), and its position insets aren't read (R7).

<details>
<summary>R1 to R8, with examples</summary>

**R1. A root fills an exact axis and wraps its content on an at-most or unbounded axis.** So it may be smaller than its room.

> With `padding(all = 4)`, at most 200 x 100, a root is 38 x 28. Exactly 200 wide and at most 300 high, with no padding, it's 200 x 20.

**R2. Margins come out of the room, on every axis, an exact axis included.** The holder places the root at its margins.

> Exactly 300 x 300, `margin(start = 6)` makes the root 294 wide, at x = 6. `margin(vertical = 5)` makes it 290 high.

**R3. An own width or height stays even when it's larger than the room**, within the root's own min and max, and the root overflows its holder. A percentage size is of the holder's maximum, and has no effect on an unbounded axis.

> Exactly 200 wide, `width(300)` is 300 and `widthPercent(50)` is 100. With an unbounded height, `heightPercent(50)` keeps the content's height, 20.

**R4. An own max applies within the room; an own min is kept even past it**, as an own width or height is ([R3](#r-a-root-layout)), and as for a child ([C3](#c-a-layouts-child)). The max bounds the box alone, with the margins outside it ([CSS `box-sizing: border-box`](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-sizing-3/#box-sizing)). An own min above an own max wins.

> Exactly 200 wide, `maxWidth(190).margin(start = 20)` is 180: the room less the margin, within the max. `minWidth(150).maxWidth(100)` is 150. Exactly or at most 200 wide, `minWidth(300)` is 300.

**R5. An aspect ratio derives the other axis only when exactly one axis is fixed**, by an own width or height or an exact constraint. The derived axis is kept within its own min and max, but not within the room. With both axes fixed, or neither, the ratio has no effect.

> Exactly 200 wide and at most 300 high, `aspectRatio(0.5f)` is 200 x 400; with `maxHeight(250)` too, it's 200 x 250. At most 300 x 300, `width(120).aspectRatio(3f)` is 120 x 40, and `width(120).height(30).aspectRatio(3f)` is 120 x 30. At most 200 x 300, `aspectRatio(2f)` alone keeps its content's 30 x 20.

**R6. Padding and border set the smallest the box can be, even past the room.**

> Exactly 200 wide, `padding(horizontal = 120)` is 240.

**R7. Position insets aren't read.** The holder places the root at its margins.

> Exactly 200 wide, `position(left = 10)` leaves the root at x = 0.

**R8. A negative size or own max has no effect, and a negative own min acts as 0**, as for a child.

> At most 300 x 300, `height(-10)` and `minHeight(-10)` each keep the content's height, 20. Exactly 200 wide, `maxWidth(-10)` is 200.

</details>

## Where a Layout differs from Yoga

Each row is a case where a `Layout`'s child or root deliberately gets a different box from the same style under Yoga. Each one follows CSS, or exists because Yoga's answer depends on something a `Layout` doesn't look at: the child's kind, the parent's alignment, the root's direction, or whether the root's size is exact. The Yoga numbers are those of Litho's `Column` and `Row`, which Yoga lays out, and the test for each row checks both. Where a row gives CSS numbers, they were measured in a browser with the equivalent CSS, and aren't part of the tests.

<details>
<summary>D1 to D13</summary>

| D2 | A child with `heightPercent(50)`, unbounded in height, inside a `Column` 500 high | Its content's height, 25 | 250: 50% of the `Column` | CSS: a percentage of an auto height is auto ([CSS 2 §10.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/CSS2/visudet.html#the-height-property)) |
| D3 | A child with `maxWidth(-10)`, in an exact room 300 wide | 300 | A child a `Column` 300 wide stretches: 0 | CSS: a negative max is invalid |
| D4 | A child with `height(40).aspectRatio(2f)`, in an exact room 300 wide | 300 x 40 | A child a `Column` 300 wide stretches: 80 x 40 | CSS: `align-self: stretch` wins over the ratio, 300 x 40 |
| D5 | A child with `aspectRatio(2f)`, at most 300 wide | 30 x 25 | A child of a `Column` 300 wide that doesn't stretch it: 50 x 25. A child of a `Row` 300 wide that doesn't stretch it: 30 x 15 | CSS block layout and a CSS row: 30 x 25. A CSS column: 50 x 25 |

| D8 | A root with `margin(start = 6)`, exactly 300 wide | 294 | 300; the margin only moves it | Matches a stretched `Column` child, 294, and CSS |
| D9 | A root with `width(120).aspectRatio(3f)`, at most 300 x 300 | 120 x 40 | 120 x 20: the height wraps the content | Yoga applies a ratio to a child, not a root: as a `Column`'s child it's 120 x 40 |

### Known limitation

**L1. A child that's a `Row` or a `Column`, whose content is wider than its room, is laid out within the room.** As the child of a `Column` that doesn't stretch it, it would keep its content's size and overflow. Laying it out again at its content's size would cost a second layout of the subtree, so it's kept as it is for now.

> A `Row` of 48 and 50, neither of which shrinks, measured at most 80 wide, is 80 wide under a `Layout`. As the child of a `Column` 80 wide that doesn't stretch it, it's 98.
