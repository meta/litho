A `Layout` is a container that positions its children itself. Its **layout function** measures the children, gives the container a size, and places each child. Use it for arrangements flexbox can't express, or that depend on how big the children turn out to be: action buttons that lose their labels when they don't fit, a row of avatars that ends in "+3", or a badge positioned from its anchor's measured size.

The children stay in the parent's Litho tree. They aren't a nested tree, so their state and incremental mount work as they do for any other child.

:::note
`Layout` and its layout-function API are marked `@ExperimentalLithoApi`. Opt in with `@OptIn(ExperimentalLithoApi::class)` on the declaration or the file that uses them, as each sample below does on its component. `Layout` is in `com.facebook.litho`, the layout-function types (`Measurable`, `Placeable`, `LayoutProps`) are in `com.facebook.litho.layout`, and `SizeConstraints` is in `com.facebook.rendercore`.
:::

This page shows how to write a `Layout`. How a `Layout` sizes its children, and how a root `Layout` is sized, is specified in [Custom Layout: Sizing Rules](custom-layout-rules.mdx), with a worked example and a test for each rule.

## When to use a Layout

| You need | Use |
|---|---|
| Children in a row or a column, with flexible sizes, spacing and alignment | `Row` and `Column` ([Flexbox](flexbox-yoga.mdx)) |
| Children stacked on top of each other | `StackLayout`, or [absolute positioning](yoga-playground.mdx#absolutely-positioned-items) |
| Different content depending only on the space available | [`RenderWithConstraints`](../custom-layout.md#kotlin-integration), or a `Layout` |
| Positions or choices that depend on the children's measured sizes, or an arrangement flexbox can't express | A `Layout` |

Prefer flexbox when it can express the layout: it's declarative, well understood, and it's what the rest of the tree uses. Coming from `RenderWithConstraints` or `@OnCreateLayoutWithSizeSpec`? See [Migrating to Layout](../custom-layout.md#migrating-to-layout).

## How a Layout works

> **Rooms go down. Sizes come up. You place.**
> A child's own style beats the room; your parent has the last word on your size.

A layout function receives the children, as `Measurable`s, and `constraints`: the space the container may occupy. It does three things:

1. **It measures the children it needs.** `child.measure(room)` lays the child out against `room`, a `SizeConstraints` the function chooses, and returns a `Placeable`. The child's own style is resolved against that room and can override it: an own `width`, `minWidth` or `maxWidth` wins over the room, so the child may come out larger or smaller than the room ([rules C1 to C12](custom-layout-rules.mdx#c-a-layouts-child)).
2. **It declares the container's size** by calling `layout(width, height) { … }`, exactly once. This **declared size** is a request: the parent may give the container another one ([P5](custom-layout-rules.mdx#p-measuring-and-placing)).
3. **It places children** inside the block passed to `layout`, which runs once the container's final size is known. A child that isn't placed occupies no space and isn't mounted.

Positions are relative to the container's **content box**, its own box less its padding and border. The padding and border also narrow the `constraints` the function receives ([P1](custom-layout-rules.mdx#p-measuring-and-placing)).

### The API at a glance

| In the layout function | What it is |
|---|---|
| `child.measure(room)` | Lays the child out against `room` and returns its `Placeable` |
| `placeable.width`, `placeable.height` | The space the child occupies: what it took, kept within the room |
| `placeable.measuredWidth`, `placeable.measuredHeight` | What the child took, margins included |
| `layout(width, height) { … }` | Declares the container's size; the block places the children |
| `placeable.placeRelative(start, top)`, `placeable.place(x, y)` | Positions a child from the start edge, or from the left |
| `constraints.copy(minWidth = 0, minHeight = 0)` | A room with the minimum dropped, so a child keeps its own size |
| `constraints.hasExactWidth`, `constraints.hasBoundedWidth` | Whether the width is exact, or has a maximum at all |
| `SizeConstraints.Infinity` | The maximum of an unbounded axis: a marker, not a size |
| `child.layoutProps(default)` | A value the child sets for this layout ([Per-child values](#per-child-values)) |
| `layoutDirection`, `16.dp.toPixels()` | The layout direction, and a dimension in pixels |

### A Placeable's width and measuredWidth

- **`measuredWidth`** is what the child took, margins included. Since a child's own size wins over the room, it may be larger or smaller than the room.
- **`width`** is `measuredWidth` kept within the room. It's the space the child occupies, and what to add up when you lay children out side by side.

`measuredHeight` and `height` are the same for the other axis. When the two differ, the child is drawn from the **start edge** of the `width` it occupies, the left edge left to right and the right edge right to left, and overflows or falls short of the other edge. It isn't centred ([P3](custom-layout-rules.mdx#p-measuring-and-placing)).

### place and placeRelative

- **`placeRelative(start, top)`** positions a child from the content box's top start corner, so it mirrors in a right-to-left layout. Use it for anything that should follow the reading direction.
- **`place(x, y)`** positions a child from the content box's top-left corner, in both directions. Use it for positions that are physical, not directional.

Things to notice:

- The `Layout` constructors for one, two and three children pass each child to the function as its own parameter; a list of children passes a `List<Measurable>`.
- The labelled buttons are measured with `maxWidth = SizeConstraints.Infinity`, an unbounded room, so they report their full width instead of shrinking, and that width is compared with `constraints.maxWidth`.
- Each room drops the minimum, `copy(minWidth = 0, minHeight = 0)`, so a child keeps its own size even when the constraints are exact (see [Common mistakes](#common-mistakes)).
- Both variants are declared up front and resolved in `render`, because a `Layout` can't add children while it measures. The icon-only buttons are measured only when the labelled ones don't fit, and the variant that isn't placed is never mounted.
- It declares `constraints.maxWidth` only when the width is bounded; under an unbounded width, it declares the chosen buttons' own width.

This is what `RenderWithConstraints` and `@OnCreateLayoutWithSizeSpec` are often used for.

Things to notice:

- `ColumnAlignment` is the per-child value, a `LayoutProps`, and `Style.columnAlignment` is its setter, named for this layout. Other containers ignore it ([Per-child values](#per-child-values)).
- The function reads each child's value with `child.layoutProps(ColumnAlignment.START)`, which returns the default when the child sets none.
- It aligns to the end by `measuredWidth`, `layoutWidth - placeable.measuredWidth`, so a child wider than the layout keeps its end at the end edge. By `width`, which is kept within the room, it would start at the start edge and overflow the end ([P3](custom-layout-rules.mdx#p-measuring-and-placing)).
- It fills an exact width, which reaches the function as `constraints.minWidth`, and is otherwise as wide as its widest child, up to the maximum width.

Each time its function runs, it measures each item once, with the minimum dropped and an unbounded height, then works out every position before it declares its size.

### 5. Share the width equally

`EqualSplitLayout` gives each child an equal share of the width, by measuring each one in an exact room of that share. Pixels that don't divide evenly go one each to the first children. When the width is unbounded, every share is as wide as the widest child, and each child keeps its own width from the start of its share.

## What the layout function may capture

A `Layout` counts as unchanged, and isn't laid out again for that reason, only when its layout function and its `deps` are unchanged. The Litho compiler plugin fills in `deps` from what a lambda written at the call site captures, so such a lambda needs nothing more: capture the values it reads, such as a spacing or a list of items, and a change to any of them lays the container out again.

<details>
<summary>Passing <code>deps</code> yourself, and functions that aren't lambdas</summary>

- Passing `deps` by hand replaces what the plugin infers. With `deps`, the container is unchanged when the function has the same class and the deps are equivalent, so `deps = emptyArray()` on a lambda that captures values keeps the old layout on screen.
- A layout function that isn't a lambda written at the call site, such as an `object` or a class, is compared with `equals`. Make a reusable function an `object`, or a `data class` of the values it reads.
- Without the compiler plugin, a lambda that captures values is a new instance on every render, so the container is never unchanged.

</details>

Settings for the arrangement, such as spacing or alignment, go in the lambda or in the props of the component that returns the `Layout`. The `Layout`'s own `style` is what it tells its parent, not its layout function.

## Per-child values

A layout that needs a value per child, such as an alignment or a column, reads it with `LayoutProps`, as [`AlignedColumnLayout`](#3-read-a-value-each-child-sets) does. Declare a final class for the value, such as the enum `ColumnAlignment : LayoutProps`, and a setter named for your layout, such as `fun Style.columnAlignment(alignment: ColumnAlignment) = layoutProps(alignment)`. In the layout function, read it with `child.layoutProps(ColumnAlignment.START)`, which returns the default when the child sets none, or with `child[ColumnAlignment::class]`, which returns null.

- Values of one class replace each other whole, and never merge: an outer component's value replaces the one the component it wraps set.
- A value set in a `Layout`'s own `style` is read by that `Layout`'s parent, not by the `Layout`.
- `com.facebook.litho.layout.LayoutProps` is unrelated to `com.facebook.litho.LayoutProps`, the Yoga props a component sets on its own node.

## How often the layout function runs

The layout function runs when the parent measures the container, and again when the constraints it's measured under change. It runs once more when the parent then gives the container a different size from the one it declared, as a stretching `Column` does ([P5](custom-layout-rules.mdx#p-measuring-and-placing) shows it). It may run on a background thread. So keep it free of side effects, and read state in `render`, capturing the value.

The children are measured only when the function asks. Measuring a child again under the same constraints in the same run returns the same `Placeable` without measuring it again, and a child that's measured but never placed is never mounted ([P6](custom-layout-rules.mdx#p-measuring-and-placing)).

## Common mistakes

**Aligning to the end by `width`.** A child larger than its room has a `width` equal to the room, so `containerWidth - placeable.width` starts it at the start edge and it overflows the end. Use `containerWidth - placeable.measuredWidth`, as [`AlignedColumnLayout`](#3-read-a-value-each-child-sets) does.

**Passing your own constraints to a child.** When the container's `constraints` are exact, so is their minimum, and a child measured under them is forced up to the full size, unless its own `width` or `maxWidth` says otherwise. Measure children under a room with a minimum of 0, such as `constraints.copy(minWidth = 0, minHeight = 0)`, unless you want them to fill it.

**Doing arithmetic on an unbounded maximum.** `SizeConstraints.Infinity` is a marker, not a size. Check `hasBoundedWidth` before you subtract from `maxWidth`, or declare it as a size.

**Using `place` for positions that should mirror.** `place(x, y)` doesn't mirror in a right-to-left layout; `placeRelative(start, top)` does.

**Measuring while placing.** Measuring a child inside the placement block doesn't compile, and forcing it through an outer receiver fails with an error that names the mistake. Measure everything in the function, and only choose among the results while placing.

**Calling `layout` twice, never calling it, or placing a child twice.** Each fails with an error that names the mistake, since a size or a position would otherwise be silently lost.

**Declaring a size the parent always overrides.** A parent that gives the container a different size from the one it declared runs the layout function a second time, at the final size. That's correct, but it costs a run. When the container fills a bounded width, declare `constraints.maxWidth`; when the width is unbounded, declare the content's width.

**Placing an earlier measurement.** You may measure a child several times, under different constraints. Placing the latest measurement is cheapest; placing an earlier one is correct, but may lay that child out again.

**Expecting flexbox props on children to work.** `flex(grow = …)` and other flexbox item props on a `Layout`'s child have no effect.

**Children from data without keys.** Give each child a stable `key`, as for any list of children (see [Keys and Identity](../state-and-side-effects/keys-and-identity.md)).

## FAQ

**Why is my child larger than the room I gave it, or not centred?** See [Find the rule by question](custom-layout-rules.mdx#find-the-rule-by-question): a child's own size wins over the room, and a child that doesn't fit is drawn from its start edge.

**What do my `Layout`'s own padding and border do?** They're outside the space the function lays out in. The function's `constraints` exclude them, and positions start inside them ([P1](custom-layout-rules.mdx#p-measuring-and-placing)).

**How is a root `Layout` sized?** It fills an exact axis, wraps its content on any other, and keeps its own `width` and `height`. See [rules R1 to R8](custom-layout-rules.mdx#r-a-root-layout).

**Can a `Layout` read a child's line metrics?** No. A `Layout` can't read a child's line metrics, such as its last line's width or its baseline, so a chat bubble that tucks its timestamp beside the last line of its text isn't expressible yet.

**Where are intrinsics?** A child has no intrinsic queries. Measure it under an unbounded or at-most room instead, as `ActionBarLayout` does; you may measure a child more than once. Litho doesn't call a `LayoutFunction`'s intrinsic overrides yet.

**Can it lay out on a background thread?** Yes, like any Litho component.

**How do I test one?** `Measurable` and `Placeable` are created only by Litho, so a layout function isn't tested on its own. Render a tree that holds it, with `LithoTestRule`, and check the layout result or the mounted views, as the samples' tests do.

## Author checklist

- [ ] Flexbox can't express this layout, or it depends on the children's measured sizes.
- [ ] Each child is measured under a room with a minimum of 0, unless it should fill the room.
- [ ] `layout(width, height)` is called exactly once, and never declares `SizeConstraints.Infinity`.
- [ ] Positions add up `width`; end alignment uses `measuredWidth`.
- [ ] Directional positions use `placeRelative`, and the layout is checked right to left and under an unbounded width.
- [ ] Every candidate is declared as a child, and the ones not chosen are left unplaced.
- [ ] The layout function captures the values it reads, and has no side effects.
- [ ] Children from data have stable keys.
